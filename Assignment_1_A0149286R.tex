\section{Two Player Normal Form Game}
Suppose not, that the expected utility from playing $b_1$ or $b_2$ is different.
WLOG, assume playing $b_1$ earns a better expected utility. That is,
\[u_2(\vec{p},b_1) > u_2(\vec{p},b_2)\]
Let $\vec{q}^* = (q_1,q_2,...,q_i,...,q_n)$.
We also have the maximum utility being
\[u_2(\vec{p},\vec{q}^*)=\sum_i q_i u_2(\vec{p},b_i)
=q_1 u_2(\vec{p},b_1) + q_2 u_2(\vec{p},b_2)
+ \sum_{i>2} q_i u_2(\vec{p},b_i)\]
But since $b_1$ earns better utility, and $q_1,q_2>0$, then
\[u_2(\vec{p},b_1) > u_2(\vec{p},b_2)\]
\[q_2 u_2(\vec{p},b_1) > q_2 u_2(\vec{p},b_2)\]
\[(q_1 + q_2) u_2(\vec{p},b_1) > 
q_1 u_2(\vec{p},b_1) + q_2 u_2(\vec{p},b_2)\]
Then we have a mixed strategy $\vec{q}^{**}=(q_1 + q_2,0,..., q_n)$
that has a better expected utility
\[ u_2(\vec{p},\vec{q}^{**})
=(q_1 + q_2) u_2(\vec{p},b_1) + \sum_{i>2} q_i u_2(\vec{p},b_i)
> u_2(\vec{p},\vec{q}^*)\]
A contradiction, since
\[u_2(\vec{p},\vec{q}^*) = \max_{\vec{q}\in\Delta_2}u_2(\vec{p},\vec{q})\]
The same argument can be used if $b_2$ instead earns a better expected utility.
Thus, the supposition is false, and player 2 earns the same expected utility
from playing $b_1$ or $b_2$.
\[u_2(\vec{p},b_1) = u_2(\vec{p},b_2)\]
